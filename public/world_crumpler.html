Colored sections (one per color) describe the Moore neighborhood of N — a tile that must itself be exactly that color. Exactly one of the 8 slots is marked X: the tile-in-question, positioned relative to N. The other 7 slots are checked against N's real neighbors. X rules instead describes X's own Moore neighborhood directly — X sits fixed at the center, no marker needed. Each row's color grid independently marks any number of colors as demanded (✓) or forbidden (✕).
